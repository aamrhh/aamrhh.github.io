Project details

How Oil Prices Affect the Malaysian Ringgit (2005–2026)

This dashboard is live

Rates update automatically every working day. Visit it to see today's rates and look up any day since 2005.

Visit the live dashboard

A live Streamlit dashboard that explains how world oil prices and the US dollar affect the Malaysian ringgit, written in plain language so anyone can follow it, not only finance experts.

  • Python
  • pandas
  • scikit-learn
  • Plotly
  • Streamlit
  • Bank Negara Malaysia data
  • FRED

Why I built this

I built this project to expand my understanding of financial markets. I wanted to learn how global factors, such as oil prices and the strength of the US dollar, affect the Malaysian ringgit, and to practise working with real market data that changes every day.

How I built it

  1. Collected the data. Daily ringgit exchange rates from Bank Negara Malaysia (through data.gov.my), plus the world oil price and a US dollar index from FRED, from 2005 to today.
  2. Cleaned and combined it in Python. Used pandas to line up the dates from different sources and to handle weekends and holidays, when no new rates are published.
  3. Analysed the relationship. Used correlation and regression (scikit-learn) to measure how closely oil and the ringgit move together, and compared the ringgit with the US dollar index and other currencies to separate the effect of a strong US dollar.
  4. Designed the dashboard. Built it in Streamlit with Plotly charts, using simple words, clear labels such as "Ringgit stronger" and short explanations under each chart.
  5. Deployed and automated it. Published it on Streamlit Community Cloud. It pulls the latest data automatically, so the numbers and summaries stay current.

Dashboard sections

What it shows

Shows whether the ringgit is stronger or weaker against the US dollar, Chinese yuan and Singapore dollar, compared with 1 month, 1 year or 5 years ago. Each card has a small trend line and a clear label such as "Ringgit stronger" or "Ringgit weaker".

Why I included it

Most news only talks about the ringgit against the US dollar. I added China and Singapore because, together with the United States, they are Malaysia's three biggest trading partners, making up about 43% of all trade in 2025 (DOSM).

What it shows

Turns exchange rates into something easy to picture: how much foreign currency RM1,000 buys today. Users can also pick any date to see that day's exchange rates and oil price.

Why I included it

Exchange rates can feel abstract, so I wanted ordinary readers to see what a change means for their own money. Bank Negara only publishes rates on working days, so if a user picks a weekend, a holiday or a day before the rate is out, the dashboard explains this and shows the latest rate before that date.

Try it yourself. Open the live dashboard, pick any date, and see that day's ringgit rates and oil price.

Look up a date

What it shows

A long-term chart of the ringgit against the US dollar and the world oil price, with buttons for the last 5 years, 12 years or since 2005. Key events are marked, such as the 2014 oil price crash, COVID-19 and the strong US dollar in 2022. Below it, four simple steps explain how a higher oil price can strengthen the ringgit.

Why I included it

This is the main question of the project. For many years, oil and the ringgit moved like a mirror: when oil rose, the ringgit got stronger. Since 2022, that link has broken, which shows that oil is not the only force moving the ringgit.

What it shows

Compares how the ringgit moved over the last month against Malaysia's top trading partners and its ASEAN neighbours, with a short written summary next to the chart.

Why I included it

When the ringgit moves against the US dollar, it is hard to tell whether the ringgit changed or the US dollar did. Comparing it with several currencies at once helps answer that question.

What it shows

Three short summaries of the last month: how much oil moved, how much the US dollar moved against other major currencies, and the ringgit's strongest and weakest days.

Why I included it

Not every visitor will read every chart. These cards give a quick answer, and their text is written automatically from the latest data, so they stay up to date.

The numbers in these screenshots were taken in October 2026. The live dashboard updates automatically, so its numbers will be different.

Limitations

  • Correlation shows that two things move together, not that one causes the other. Interest rates, the US dollar and investor mood also move the ringgit.
  • Bank Negara publishes exchange rates on working days only, so weekends and public holidays have no new values.
  • Oil and US dollar index data from FRED can lag a few days behind the ringgit data.

Malaysian Blood Donation Trend Dashboard

A five-page Power BI dashboard that explores when, where and how Malaysians donate blood, using daily national data from January 2020 to May 2026.

  • Power BI
  • Python
  • pandas
  • NumPy
  • DOSM open data

Why I built this

A steady blood supply depends on a steady flow of donors, but donations are not spread evenly across the year, the week or the country. I wanted to find out when and where Malaysians donate most, which blood types are most and least common, and whether weekends and public holidays change how people donate. These patterns could help plan blood donation drives at the right time and place.

How I built it

  1. Defined the questions. Listed what I wanted to learn: the overall trend, differences between states, the share of each blood type, and the effect of weekends and public holidays.
  2. Collected the data. Used open data from the Department of Statistics Malaysia (DOSM) on daily blood donations by state and blood type.
  3. Cleaned and prepared it in Python. Used pandas and NumPy to check for missing values, fix data types and reshape the data. Added new columns for the day of the week and public holiday names, so the dashboard could compare normal days with holidays.
  4. Built the dashboard in Power BI. Designed five pages that each answer one question, with slicers, a map and filters so users can explore the data themselves.
  5. Summarised the findings. Wrote up the key insights and the limits of the data in the project README.

Dashboard pages

What it shows

Shows the overall donation trend from January 2020 to May 2026, with the total number of donations (about 4 million) and the daily average (about 1,520 donations a day). A year slider lets users focus on any period.

Key finding

Donations stayed steady during the COVID-19 years of 2020 and 2021, then rose sharply in 2022, which suggests a strong recovery after the pandemic. They remained high until 2025.

The drop in 2026 is not a fall in donations. The data for 2026 only covers January to May.

What it shows

Compares donations across states using an interactive map and a ranked bar chart. Selecting a state on the map shows its total donations and blood type breakdown, and a date range filter narrows the period.

Key finding

W.P. Kuala Lumpur records far more donations than any other state, at over 1.2 million. Sabah and Sarawak also rank higher than several states in Peninsular Malaysia.

What it shows

Shows the share of each blood type, how donations for each type changed over the years, and the blood type mix in every state.

Key finding

Type O makes up the largest share at 42.62%, followed by Type B (27.06%), Type A (24.66%) and Type AB (5.66%). The mix is very similar across all states, and the small share of Type AB mainly reflects how rare it is in the population.

What it shows

Looks at which days of the week and which public holidays have the highest average donations.

Key finding

Sunday has the highest average donations, followed by Saturday, while Monday and Friday are the lowest. Donations are generally higher on public holidays than on normal weekdays, with Labour Day leading, followed by National Day, Wesak Day, Malaysia Day and Deepavali.

What it shows

Lets users pick any month and year to see the total donations, the daily average and the public holidays that fell in that month.

Key finding

For example, May 2020 recorded about 40,000 donations, with a daily average of about 1,280, in a month that included Hari Raya Aidilfitri, Labour Day and Wesak Day.

Limitations

  • Blood types are not split by Rhesus factor (positive or negative).
  • Only official public holidays are included, not extra or replacement holidays.
  • There is no data on blood demand, so the dashboard cannot show shortages.
  • The data covers donation counts only, not donor age, gender or other details.
